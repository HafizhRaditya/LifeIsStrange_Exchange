## Corrections
Elena Ward is 29. This is the load-bearing version: it places her at 18–19 in 2013, the same graduating cohort as Max Caulfield, which is what makes "she knew Max personally" work.
Chief Barry Deskowsky is 56. The "48" in his appearance header is dead.
Amber Clarke uses the loud build only — brash, theatrical, neon streetwear, LED ring-light phone case, live-narrates humiliations. The "soft sweater / quiet observer" description is dead.
Jake Cross captains football. Nolan Burgh captains basketball. Their rivalry is cross-sport and social, not positional.
Every stray "Ethan" in the Crosshairs material is Jake. The single stray "Logan" in Luke Pierce's profile is Luke. Ethan now belongs to exactly one character: Mr. Ethan Rowe.
## Standing Format Rules
Fiz never speaks in dialogue_sequence. His spoken lines exist only inside choices. His internal monologue is authored, flagged is_internal_thought, and wrapped in parentheses.
Place and time belong to the Creative Director. Locations proposed here are placeholders and may be overridden by any [ ] tag.
No real-world brands. In-universe platforms only: Prism, Glimpse, Flicker, Echo, Nexus, DeepThread, Aura, ViewGrid.
Rated M. Swearing is natural to character, never decorative.
## The Two Standing Creative Rulings
The Anomaly — Ambiguous.
Nothing in the season is ever stated to be impossible. What is impossible is Fiz's reading of it. Every anomalous beat is written with a mundane explanation held in reserve — logged below in the Anomaly Register — so nothing has to be walked back in Episodes 3–5.
Olivia Calder — Played Straight.
Zero tells. No coded lines, no lingering beats, no double meanings. Everything she says in Episodes 1–3 is sincere. The Episode 4 reveal has to hurt on a first pass and hold up on a second precisely because she was not performing.
## Anomaly Register (Private — Writer's Reference)
The mundane answer behind each anomaly. Veto any of these before it becomes load-bearing.
Ep1 — Fog reverses for 11 frames in continuous footage. Mundane answer: the clip was not continuous. It was re-encoded and stitched by someone with access to the media lab archive, and the timecode was rewritten to hide the seam. Fiz is looking at a forgery, not a phenomenon.
Alice Carter — "a photo moved on its own." Mundane answer: two near-identical prints in the same file, swapped between viewings.
Sienna Morales — "a picture that shouldn't exist." Mundane answer: it exists. The date on it is wrong because the camera's internal clock was never set.
Luke Pierce — "something fell from the sky into the bay." Mundane answer: a marine flare from a salvage operation Cross Industries never logged.
Ms. Leighton — pressure and magnetic anomalies, 2013. Mundane answer: real data, real instruments, and a monitoring station that was already failing before the storm.
Mr. Rowe — "time skips in recorded data." Mundane answer: he analysed the same forgeries. This is the thread that eventually points at the forger.
## Season Spine
## Episode 1 — Blast For The Future
Fiz's observer life, the casual cruelty of Zoey and Jake's clique, the deepening bond with Riley. Ends when his own footage does something footage cannot do.
## Episode 2 — Good Times
The inner circle chases normalcy against escalating Glimpse and ViewGrid harassment. Emotional core: Fiz teaches Riley to swim in the Blackwell pool at night. Ends on Richard Jones and Gerald Cross, overheard.
## Episode 3 — The Place Beyond Us
Observers become investigators. Restricted files, Cross Industries, the Prescott bankruptcy. Ends in a physical confrontation with the Crosshairs that leaves the group exposed.
## Episode 4 — Rendered
The evidence leaks. Blackwell's hierarchy collapses. The twist: Olivia Calder is orchestrating the present-day psychological campaign.
## Episode 5 — Lost and Found
A psychological showdown with no powers to escape into. Final choice: publish the unedited truth and destroy the rebuilt town, or bury it and join the silence.

## Notes for the Engine Build
Relationship values are integers, unbounded, initialised at 0. Episode 5's epilogue reads them directly; no other scene should gate on an absolute threshold without a design pass.
consequence_id values are unique across the season and safe to use as flag keys.
Choice blocks are never terminal — every scene has at least one dialogue entry after its final choice so the player is never left on a dead branch.
Internal thoughts should render visually distinct from spoken dialogue. They carry most of the characterisation and all of the unreliable narration.
The Episode 1 anomaly must be shown, not described — the eleven frames need to actually play. If the engine cannot do frame-stepped video, the scene needs a redesign, not a workaround.
